Kimi (subscription) kimi¶

Moonshot's Kimi coding backend spoken with an agent-owned Kimi subscription. Routes are kimi/<model> over plain openai-chat; the pinned model is kimi/kimi-for-coding. Who may spend the account is the entitlement decision; the account itself is a subscription. Not to be confused with moonshot, the API-key platform route.

How we sign in¶

Kimi has no local CLI that can mint or refresh a subscription credential: the grant comes out of the kimi.com OAuth sign-in in a real browser. On this fleet that browser is Weles's, on Weles's own host: Weles exposes the sign-in as POST /reauth on its worker API, drives its stored sign-in row for the account, and writes the minted grant into the Skarbiec vault at provider:kimi:<subscription>. The proof is the vault bundle at a higher revision and a refresh that answers refreshed — never the login's own exit status.

Drivers: brama subscription sign-in kimi --reason "<why>" on the host Weles runs on (cli), the renewal loop, Brama Desktop, or a signed donation through POST /v1/subscription-pool (donation).

The grant on disk¶

The simplest of the three subscription shapes — a flat OAuth object:

{
  "access_token":  "<short-lived bearer>",
  "refresh_token": "<rotating refresh token>",
  "expires_at":    1756150000
}

expires_at is epoch seconds, written back on every refresh from the token endpoint's expires_in; the gateway also accepts expiresAt, expires and expiry, and reads the access token's JWT exp claim when the blob states nothing (src/gateway/oauth_refresh.rs). The item carries the discovery tags (brama:subscription, brama:agent:<agent>, brama:provider:kimi, brama:id:<id>); losing the agent tag makes the account unroutable and /readyz names it under unroutable_accounts.

How a request presents it¶

At final use the gateway redeems the vault item through the local entitlements router, reduces it to the access token, and sends Authorization: Bearer <access_token> — nothing provider-specific beyond that. No copy of the token outlives the request.

Refresh¶

Ahead of expiry the gateway posts grant_type=refresh_token with the stored refresh token and the public client id 17e5f671-d194-4dfb-9706-5516cb48c098 to https://auth.kimi.com/api/oauth/token, form-encoded, bounded at 15 seconds and a 64 KiB answer. The sweep runs every BRAMA_CREDENTIAL_REFRESH_INTERVAL_SECS (60) and refreshes every active credential expiring within BRAMA_CREDENTIAL_REFRESH_SKEW_SECS (300), single-flighted per subscription; a token is considered dead 60 seconds before its stated expiry. The refresh token rotates on issue, so a refreshed grant that cannot be written back to the vault is a failed refresh. brama subscription refresh kimi --reason "<why>" forces the same path now and journals the reason. Refreshing costs no plan quota.

When the provider disowns the grant¶

A refused refresh is definitive only on the provider's own evidence — invalid_grant, invalid_token, revoked, unauthorized_client, or a 401/403 with no stated reason. That records credential.state: needs_reauthorization with the provider's sentence as cause, blocks the account for 30 minutes, lists it burnt, and waits for the sign-in above; everything else is transient and left for the next sweep (subscription, runbook).

Plan usage¶

Kimi publishes a free usage report at https://api.kimi.com/coding/v1/usages, issued to exactly the OAuth bearer the chat route already presents — reading it costs no quota. The ledger records each window with its instant and source (subscription).
